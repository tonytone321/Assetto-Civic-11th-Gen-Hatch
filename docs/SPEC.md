Mission
Build the engineering foundation for a high-fidelity Assetto Corsa (AC) car mod of one specific vehicle:
2024 Honda Civic Hatchback Sport Touring, 1.5 L turbo, 6-speed manual, front-wheel drive, Canadian market, Sonic Grey Pearl.
The end goal is a mod clearly more accurate than a typical community road car: exact exterior and full cockpit, working digital cluster, factory-correct physics, and a tasteful cat-back exhaust sound whose overrun burble occurs only when it physically would. I will handle ksEditor, the KN5 export, the FMOD bank build and in-game testing. You do everything else, and you create real files, not instructions.
This run is Phase 1 only: research and a sourced engineering database. No Blender modeling, textures, AC physics files, FMOD project, CFD, LODs or FBX. Everything later is built on this data, so a truthful gap is worth more than a plausible guess, and accuracy matters more than speed. Later phases, for context: Blender model, materials and working cluster, AC physics generated from this database, state-driven sound, validation and handoff. The stock car is the baseline, and the cat-back changes sound only.

Rules

1. Exact identity. Do not mix in data or details from the Civic Sedan, Si, Type R, lower hatchback trims, 2.0 L cars, CVT cars, 2025-and-later facelift cars, the 10th generation or markets outside North America, unless you record evidence that the part is shared. Where US and Canadian specifications differ, record both and select the Canadian one. Data from 2022 and 2023 cars with the same body, engine and gearbox counts when the record says so and you have checked for model-year changes.
2. Sources, not memory. Your recollection of a specification is a lead, not a source, and so is a search snippet. Open the page and find the value before recording it. Render JavaScript-heavy pages in a headless browser, and extract PDF text with the page number.
3. Unknown is a valid answer. If a value has not turned up after the obvious primary sources and about five distinct searches, record null with what you tried and how I could measure it, and move on. Covering every parameter beats exhausting one. Hard points, damper curves, spring rates, inertia and lift are mostly unpublished, so expect to mark them estimated or unknown, and never dress up an estimate.
4. Derived numbers come from code, never typing, and reports are generated from the data so they cannot drift.
5. Report only what happened. Say plainly when a site was blocked, a tool was not run or a push failed, and say exactly what is missing when you are stuck. Do not substitute, mock or guess, and do not work around logins, paywalls or access blocks.
6. Legitimate material only. Nothing from games or other mods. Third-party photos, video and audio are cataloged by link, with any downloaded copies in a git-ignored cache.
7. The repository is your memory. Write findings to disk as you go, keep a short log per domain in docs/research_log/, and commit and push after each domain, because a cloud sandbox can be reclaimed.

Evidence format
Every value is a record: value, unit, status (confirmed, estimated or unknown), class, source id, locator (page or timestamp), evidence (the verbatim text stating the value, or where it sits in an image), the figure as printed, applicability (which year, market, trim and gearbox the source describes), confidence, range (required for classes D to F), how_to_measure (for estimates and unknowns) and notes. A parameter may hold several candidate records.
Classes: A manufacturer or government. B direct measurement. C reputable third party, such as instrumented tests and dyno runs. D derived by script. E engineering estimate. F fallback assumption.
Precedence: my own verified measurement, then A, then B from other cars and C, then D, E, F. Keep the losing candidates. When credible sources disagree, keep both, say which you selected and why, and list it in docs/CONFLICTS.md. Never average silently.
Scripts are small, modular Python in scripts/: resolve.py picks the effective record per parameter into vehicle_data/_resolved.json; derive.py computes derived values; validate_db.py fails on missing fields, unsourced A to C records, derived values that do not match recomputation and failed cross-checks; make_report.py generates the reports.
Units are SI. Vehicle coordinates: origin on the ground at the centerline below the front axle, +X right, +Y forward, +Z up, meters.
Method
Work domain by domain, using subagents for independent domains: give each the record format and the files it owns, and have it return a short summary. Work through all eleven domains before integrating, without pausing to ask whether to continue. Find a second independent source for high-impact values (mass, power and torque, gear ratios, tire, wheel and brake sizes, dimensions, test results).
Trust, in order: Honda Canada and US specification pages, press kits, owner's manuals and parts catalogs; government data (Transport Canada, NHTSA, NRCan, EPA); tire and wheel makers; instrumented tests naming trim, gearbox and tires; stock dyno runs with dyno type and conditions; then forums, listings and videos, good for leads and photos but weak as the only source for a number.
Research domains
Each domain writes topic-named JSON files in vehicle_data/ (transmission.json and so on), plus any path named below.

1. Identity. Model year, trim, body, market, plant, chassis, engine and gearbox codes, drivetrain, options, interior configuration. Confirm this trim, engine and gearbox was sold in Canada for 2024 and that Sonic Grey Pearl was offered, with its paint code. List what visibly distinguishes Sport Touring from lower hatchback trims, what the manual changes in the cabin, and every confirmed US/Canada and 2022 to 2024 difference.
2. Dimensions. Length, width with and without mirrors, height, wheelbase, front and rear track, ground clearance and overhangs, with manufacturer figures as the authority. Unpublished proportions (ride height, arch positions, windshield base, roof peak, hood height, hatch angle, bumper extents, mirror position) are measured by script from straight-on, long-lens photos scaled by wheelbase and wheel diameter, recording the image, pixel coordinates and error. Nothing by eye.
3. Mass and inertia. Curb mass for this trim and gearbox (Canadian if available), GVWR and axle ratings, fuel capacity and mass, the fuel level assumed, and front/rear distribution or corner weights. If distribution is unpublished, derive a range and show the derivation; never assume 50/50. CG position and pitch, yaw and roll inertia come from this car's mass and dimensions through published empirical relationships such as NHTSA inertial-parameter data, with formulas and ranges, never copied from another AC car.
4. Engine and turbo. Code, displacement, bore, stroke, compression, valvetrain, injection, turbo and intercooler layout, rated power and torque with rpm, idle, redline, fuel cut and limiter behavior, fuel, and estimated engine and flywheel inertia. Beware figures for the Si and other L15 variants. Keep each stock dyno run (this car, else an equivalent stock 1.5T manual hatch) as its own file in engine_curves/ with dyno type, correction and gear, digitizing chart images by script. Build engine_curve.csv by script (crank and wheel torque and power by rpm, assumed loss, source), keeping the real boost onset, plateau and taper: no cosmetic smoothing, no invented points. If dynos and rated figures disagree, keep both and explain. Boost onset, target and taper against rpm, wastegate behavior, transient response and torque management, ideally from stock datalogs. Expect a small, responsive turbo; re-check anything implying heavy lag.
5. Drivetrain. All six ratios, reverse and final drive. gear_speed_table.csv gives speed per 1000 rpm, at redline and at the limiter in each gear, generated by script from the ratios and tire rolling circumference and cross-checked against a real observation such as rpm at 100 km/h in sixth. Ratios are never adjusted later to hit a target. Clutch size and capacity, flywheel type and inertia, shift time, rpm drop between gears, rev hang (how long, how much, when) and driveline inertia. Differential: open, mechanical LSD or brake-based, with evidence; do not assume the Si's LSD.
6. Steering and suspension. Steering type, ratio and whether it is variable, turns lock to lock, maximum wheel angles, turning circle and wheel diameter; cross-check ratio and lock against the turning circle and estimate Ackermann. Confirm the architecture from parts diagrams, repair videos and underside photos: front strut layout, every rear multi-link member by name and function, and spring, damper and anti-roll-bar placement. Hard points in vehicle coordinates, each marked confirmed (dimensioned source), estimated (generated by script from stated constraints with a range per coordinate, never hand-typed) or unknown; report the count of each. Factory alignment with tolerances. Spring rates, motion ratios, wheel rates, ride frequencies, travel and bump stops. Damper force-velocity data, or conservative derived curves marked as inferred. Anti-roll-bar diameters, lever geometry and computed stiffness.
7. Wheels, tires, brakes and aero. The factory Sport Touring wheel: diameter, width, offset, bolt pattern, center bore, mass, part number, finish and spoke design. The factory tire: make, model, size, load and speed rating, OEM variant, diameter, section width, loaded radius, rolling circumference, mass and independent test data. Brakes: rotor diameter and thickness, caliper type and pistons front and rear, booster type, ABS and computed bias. Aero: Cd, frontal area, CdA, underbody, spoiler and cooling openings; lift and balance only if published, otherwise unknown.
8. Performance targets (validation_targets.json). From several instrumented tests: 0-60 and 5-60 mph, 0-100 km/h, passing times, quarter mile and trap speed, braking distances, skidpad and top speed. Record the publication, the exact car (year, trim, gearbox, tires), test weight, weather and rollout. Targets are ranges across tests, never one outlier.
9. Visual references (references/manifest.json, references/COVERAGE.md, paint.json, dashboard.json). For each item: URL, owner, view, lens character, year and trim shown, differences from the target, usefulness. Prefer press, then dealer, then straight-on owner photos and walkaround video; distrust wide-angle phone shots. Confirm each shows a 2022 to 2024 Sport Touring hatchback and, for cabin and cluster, a manual car. Cover every exterior side and detail (roof, lights, grille, badges, spoiler, mirrors, wheels), the whole interior from dash and cluster to shifter, pedals, seats, door cards and cargo area, the engine bay, the underbody and the brakes. COVERAGE.md rates each area sufficient, thin or missing and says what cannot yet be modeled accurately. Paint: Honda's code, then base color, flake, pearl shift, clearcoat and roughness estimated with ranges from photos in sun, shade, overcast and indoor light. Cluster: type and size, layout, how speed, rpm, fuel, temperature, gear or shift indication, odometer and trip are shown, every warning and indicator lamp, Canadian units.
10. Audio (audio/reference_database.json, audio/OBSERVATIONS.md). Catalog stock and cat-back clips of 2022 to 2024 manual 1.5T hatchbacks (MagnaFlow, Remark, Borla and others): URL, the car and modifications as stated, microphone position, events with timestamps (idle, load, coast, lift, shifts, turbo, burble, pops), quality and license. Prefer clips that list their modifications; down-weight unknown modifications, catless downpipes, crackle tunes, anti-lag, CVTs, music, wind and clipping. My reference for the exhaust character is https://www.youtube.com/shorts/BDuJz3FTOpU : establish the car, exhaust and modifications if documented and, as far as the material allows, the rpm range and the burble (pop count, spacing, duration, tone, level, delay after the throttle closes). Analyze audio by script only when you have it as a file I supplied or media you can legitimately access. If you could not, say so; never describe a sound you did not analyze. Online audio is reference only, and nothing is redistributed without a confirmed license. OBSERVATIONS.md records when overrun burble really occurs (throttle state, rpm, preceding load, shift phase) and how it varies.
11. Platform (docs/PLATFORM_CONSTRAINTS.md). Cite each point, from the official AC pipeline documents if I supplied them and the Custom Shaders Patch (CSP) documentation: the files a loadable car needs; node naming; LOD and collider structure (believed: one KN5 per LOD plus a collider KN5); FBX and texture requirements; instrument options; the animation pipeline; the audio pipeline (FMOD Studio 1.08.12, the SDK template, the parameters the game supplies); what CSP Lua car scripts can read and trigger, and from which version; how AC models torque and boost (believed: turbo sections in engine.ini, no turbo.ini); axis conventions; which steps need my Windows PC. Mark what you could not confirm.
